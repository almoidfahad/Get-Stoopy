# Owner decision: COD collection by delivery

The owner selected the following for a multi-rider order: each rider collects the price of products in their own delivery, and the single order-level customer delivery fee is collected by the **last successful delivery**. This complements, but does not replace, the highest-slab-once fee rule and Purbadhala ৳30 order-level cap (`docs/181`, `docs/185`). It does not say that each rider collects the full order total, or that every rider collects a delivery fee.

This decision is **not yet implemented**. In particular, the current system has one assignment per suborder, not an implementation of grouping several store pickups into one rider itinerary, and its legacy COD finance path records the entire order amount on each delivery. Existing COD safeguards and the production order-creation gate remain in place. Neither the arithmetic drafts nor this document authorize cash collection or live COD.

For the specific case where an earlier rider has delivered and the last rider's delivery fails, the owner chose to **retry the last delivery** and collect the one delivery charge on its successful retry; the charge is not collected before that success. This is not an authorization to bill an earlier rider or create an extra charge. What happens if the retry never succeeds, the buyer cancels, or delivery is partially completed remains undecided.

A later failure can make an earlier delivery retrospectively the last successful one, after that rider has already left. The system cannot safely infer the final cash payer from a completion timestamp without a defined finalization/coordination protocol and a policy for failures, cancellations and partial handoffs. Concurrent riders also need a durable, atomic and idempotent way to prevent collecting the fee twice. Further decisions and review are required for undispatched or pending deliveries, cancellation, customer-visible payable amounts, discount/tax/other-charge allocation, cash shortages and disputes, rider settlement, and refunds/returns. The order-level cap must continue to cover all applicable customer delivery charges. Avoid charging customers extra or creating a debt by assumption.

A production implementation additionally needs binding the authoritative fee and product totals to the order and deliveries, a consistent customer/rider view before handing over cash, a reviewed change to database invariants and finance journals, proof and audit/reconciliation of amounts actually collected, identity and access controls, route and serviceability verification, a safe isolated database and live infrastructure, and end-to-end concurrency/recovery testing. No migration, deployment, provider call, or hosted data change is made by recording this decision.
