# Candidate multi-store allocation arithmetic

The arithmetic-only pricing foundation in `docs/163` expects components that are already allocated to vendor/store groups. This increment adds `packages/core/src/checkout-allocation.ts`, an isolated **candidate** largest-remainder proportional allocator. It is not exported by the core package barrel, is not called by checkout or payment, and does not establish a discount, tax, delivery fee or refund allocation policy.

Inputs must explicitly supply a nonnegative signed-BIGINT-range total in minor units, 1–50 unique canonical lowercase group identifiers, and nonnegative integer weights with at least one positive weight. Missing/invalid values and duplicates fail closed; zero total is allowed only with a valid positive total weight. BigInt is used throughout, including products and the aggregate weight, which may exceed signed BIGINT. Each group's floor share is calculated exactly, and leftover minor units go to the largest fractional remainders; ties use ascending ASCII group IDs, independently of request order. Results are copied and sorted by group ID, nonnegative and sum exactly to the original total. Identifiers must be server-assigned, stable and non-sensitive.

Synthetic tests cover ties and permutation, zero weights, malformed data, a sum and products above 64-bit range, 1–50 groups, floor/ceiling bounds and sum conservation, and one illustrative discount reconciliation using the arithmetic calculator. The example is not an approved commercial rule. Largest remainder is just one possible method and is not monotonic for every change in total (the Alabama paradox); it has no per-group cap, jurisdiction, currency, tax-inclusivity, eligibility or refund rules. The caller must validate caps and provenance, choose approved weights and method, snapshot all inputs and outputs, and separately design partial cancellations/refunds and concurrent policy changes. No code chooses when this method is appropriate. Approving customer delivery pricing, tax treatment and allocations remains necessary before production pricing. No database, live data, quote, checkout, deployment or money movement changed.
